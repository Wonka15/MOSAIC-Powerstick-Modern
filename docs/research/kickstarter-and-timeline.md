## What MOSAIC was presented as

A July 21, 2015 Android Authority article described MOSAIC as a combined portable charger and wireless media-storage device designed by PowerStick. The article reported these campaign-era advertised features:

- 4,500 mAh internal battery.
- 32 GB, 64 GB, 128 GB, and 256 GB flash-storage options.
- Built-in Wi-Fi for sharing/streaming media to as many as seven devices.
- Ability to connect an external drive or USB storage and expand up to 2 TB.
- A companion app for managing files on the device.
- USB Type-C connection for moving files to/from a computer.
- Preview pricing reported as starting at $100 for 32 GB and reaching $250 for a 256 GB edition.
- October 2015 was reported as the *planned* delivery period. This is a forecast in launch coverage, not proof that all rewards shipped then.

**Source:** [Android Authority / AIVAnet, “Mosaic battery, storage and WiFi pack hits Kickstarter,” July 21, 2015](https://www.aivanet.com/2015/07/mosaic-battery-storage-and-wifi-pack-hits-kickstarter/).

## Timeline and later hands-on evidence

### July 21, 2015 — Kickstarter launch coverage

Android Authority reported that MOSAIC had landed on Kickstarter and described the product's planned battery, storage, Wi-Fi sharing, app, and external-storage features. The article also embeds a campaign video hosted on Kickstarter's media CDN.

### January 26, 2016 — hands-on review

[Gear Diary's review, “Powerstick's Mosaic Powerstream Will Let You Have Expandable Storage”](https://geardiary.com/2016/01/26/powersticks-mosaic-powerstream-will-let-you-have-expandable-storage/), describes a 16 GB review unit and reports hands-on use of the wireless file-sharing experience. The reviewer says the unit was supplied by the manufacturer.

The review mentions a 7,000 mAh battery, while the 2015 launch coverage and the physical PSTR2 label recorded in this repository indicate 4,500 mAh. It also mentions a 16 GB unit while the 2015 launch article lists 32 GB as its smallest advertised Kickstarter configuration. These differences may reflect different product variants, prototype/review hardware, or reporting error. **Do not assume the figures describe the same exact hardware revision.** Record the label and model number when comparing a physical unit.

This is a physical-unit observation, separate from campaign advertising and press reporting. Keep the device label as the source for this specific unit.
